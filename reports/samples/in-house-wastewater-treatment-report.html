In-house wastewater treatment: what the source states

Wastewater generated on campus is treated in four sewage treatment plants (total capacity 1,943 kL/day) that run continuously, follow a written SOP displayed at the plant, and return treated water to campus use instead of discharging it. Two are conventional aeration-based plants and two are chemical-free Bio STPs.

The Bio pipe STP works without chemicals or mechanical aeration, generates no excess sludge and is odour-free, with a compact, modular design and lower power use than aeration-based plants. Filtered water is sampled daily at the softener outlet and sent monthly to NABL-certified laboratories.

Treatment process

  1. Preliminary treatment: Bar screen and oil and grease chamber.
  2. Pumping and primary settling: Raw sewage pumps and primary settling tank.
  3. Biological (aeration) treatment: MLSS verified every 8 hours.
  4. Sludge management: Recirculation and sludge holding tank.
  5. Tertiary treatment: Pressure sand filter, activated carbon filter and softener.
  6. Disinfection: Chlorine dosing to maintain 0.2 ppm residual chlorine.
